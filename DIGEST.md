## Notable this week

### EU 21st Russia sanctions package: 11 crypto platforms banned and first country-level crypto-sector kill-switch power
*EU · 10 June 2026 · High impact*
> **So what:** A country-level crypto-sector ban is structurally more powerful than entity-level listing — a single EC designation decision cuts an entire jurisdiction's CASP ecosystem off from EU-regulated markets, with no grandfathering for existing counterparty relationships. Banks should stress-test exposure now: any correspondent banking, liquidity, or CASP counterparty flows routed through Turkish, Emirati, or Kazakh platforms could become sanctioned exposure without a specific entity being pre-listed. The 11 new platform bans require an immediate counterparty sweep. This is distinct from the 20th package's blanket ban on Russian and Belarusian CASPs — the 21st extends the weapon to Russia's intermediary jurisdictions.
Source: [OCCRP — EU Plans Massive 21st Sanctions Package to Target Russian Banks and Crypto](https://www.occrp.org/en/news/eu-plans-massive-21st-sanctions-package-to-target-russian-banks-and-crypto)

### 21 days to MiCA deadline: only ~17% of pre-MiCA VASPs licensed — banks face imminent CASP counterparty cliff
*EU · 7 June 2026 · High impact*
> **So what:** Roughly 83% of the existing CASP universe will be operating in breach of EU law from July 1 unless they wind down or receive last-minute authorisation. For banks, any CASP counterparty relationship not confirmed as an authorised CASP in ESMA's register is a live compliance risk today — not a future risk. Run an immediate pass of all active CASP counterparties (including omnibus accounts, payment routing and liquidity providers) against the ESMA Interim MiCA Register. The 10 EU jurisdictions with zero authorisations signal uneven enforcement — monitor NCA-level post-July 1 actions. Existing item feed-mica-casp-july1-deadline covers the legal framework; this data quantifies the counterparty cliff.
Source: [ESMA — Interim MiCA Register (updated 4 June 2026)](https://www.esma.europa.eu/esmas-activities/digital-finance-and-innovation/markets-crypto-assets-regulation-mica)

### FBI IC3 2025: crypto fraud hits record $11.4B — half of all US cybercrime losses
*US · 7 April 2026 · High impact*
> **So what:** Crypto-enabled investment fraud is now the largest single US cybercrime loss category by dollar value, and its share is growing. For banks, two operational implications follow: (1) transaction monitoring should be calibrated for higher SAR volumes from pig-butchering and trust-investment typologies as the loss curve steepens; (2) the $7.7B senior-loss figure creates specific obligations under financial-exploitation frameworks — front-line staff should treat rapid large crypto transfers by older customers as a red flag requiring positive confirmation of purpose. Operation Level Up's model also signals FBI expectation that financial institutions actively cooperate to interrupt fraud in progress, not just file retrospective SARs.
Source: [FBI IC3 — 2025 Internet Crime Report (PDF)](https://www.ic3.gov/AnnualReport/Reports/2025_IC3Report.pdf)

### FinCEN Section 311 severs Swiss MBaer Merchant Bank from US system — FINMA orders liquidation
*US · 26 February 2026 · High impact*
> **So what:** Section 311 is the functional equivalent of a dollar-clearing death sentence — MBaer's banking licence was gone within days of the NPRM. For UK/EU banks, the lesson is that a small private bank with concentrated high-risk client exposure and lax controls can be severed from the dollar system with no grandfathering period. Review any correspondent banking or sub-custodian relationships with Swiss private banks that have material CIS/Russia/Iran-nexus client books — the MBaer precedent signals FinCEN is prepared to use its most severe tool against non-US banks.
Source: [Treasury — FinCEN proposes cutting off Swiss bank MBaer's US financial system access](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0408)

### Binance on brink of pan-European MiCA CASP licence via Greece — world's largest exchange set to enter EU AML supervisory perimeter before July 1
*EU · 4 June 2026 · Medium impact*
> **So what:** Binance securing MiCA authorisation would be the highest-profile single CASP approval under the new EU framework — and the most complicated for bank counterparty teams to process. MiCA CASP authorisation establishes an EU legal baseline but is not a clean AML bill of health: Binance is simultaneously under active DOJ investigation for $1.7B in Iran and Russia flows (see feed-binance-doj-iran-probe) and operating under a court-ordered US compliance monitorship from its 2023 $4.3B settlement. EU authorisation via a fast-tracked national review does not cancel that exposure. For banks, the correct risk posture on Binance is 'pending MiCA — enhanced scrutiny warranted' even if the formal authorisation arrives before July 1: treat it as a lower-quality counterparty assurance than exchanges with clean enforcement histories and multi-year MiCA supervisory track records.
Source: [AML Intelligence — Binance likely to secure pan-European MiCA licence in Greece](https://www.amlintelligence.com/2026/06/news-binance-likely-to-secure-pan-european-mica-licence-in-greece/)
