### Federal Reserve issues GENIUS Act CIP proposed rule — Warsh abstains, secondary-market gap persists, 60-day comment period pushes past July 18 deadline
*US · 18 June 2026 · High impact*
> **So what:** The Fed's rule closes the last institutional gap in the GENIUS Act CIP architecture — completing a five-agency suite (OCC, FDIC, NCUA, FinCEN/OFAC, Fed). Three bank compliance implications: (1) BHC-subsidiary stablecoin issuers now face the highest compliance bar of any PPSI pathway — Fed supervision plus bank-equivalent CIP obligations make Fed-supervised issuers the most credible counterparty tier for AML purposes; update counterparty risk tiering to distinguish Fed-supervised PPSIs from OCC-chartered and state-licensed peers. (2) The 60-day comment period (closing mid-August) means the Fed's final rule will land on the January 18, 2027 backstop date, not the accelerated November 2026 scenario; the multi-agency effective-date map is now split. (3) Warsh's unexplained abstention from Powell's proposal — the first meaningful Fed GENIUS Act split — introduces genuine uncertainty about the final rule's scope; track whether Warsh signals preference for supervisory flexibility (lighter CIP for non-bank PPSIs) or Barr's secondary-market extension during the comment period. ### SEC and CFTC issue landmark joint crypto asset classification — five-part token taxonomy ends enforcement-by-litigation era
*US · 17 March 2026 · High impact*
> **So what:** The five-part taxonomy is the first authoritative basis in US law for classifying crypto assets — ending five years of SEC enforcement-by-litigation that left financial institutions with no clear framework for counterparty risk on crypto holdings. Three direct compliance implications: (1) banks can now tier counterparty and custody risk frameworks against defined asset categories rather than applying blanket conservatism; (2) the investment-contract clarification — that a non-security asset ceases to be one once issuer obligations are fulfilled — requires ongoing classification monitoring, not a one-time call; (3) combined with the GENIUS Act stablecoin framework (already in force), the SEC-CFTC taxonomy completes the core US regulatory classification map, narrowing residual uncertainty to exchange-level jurisdiction questions the CLARITY Act (still pending; see feed-clarity-act-july4-dead) is intended to resolve.

### FATF offshore VASP report: 'nested account' abuse lets unlicensed VASPs hide as retail customers — only 46% of jurisdictions apply activity-based licensing
*Global · 11 March 2026 · High impact*
> **So what:** The 'nested account' finding is the highest-priority operational risk for banks operating in the crypto space: an unlicensed offshore VASP posing as a private individual retail customer defeats both VASP-relationship due diligence and Travel Rule obligations, making the bank an unwitting correspondent for unlicensed crypto activity. Two controls are directly indicated: (1) behavioural profiling of apparent retail customers for high-frequency, high-value, multi-counterparty crypto transfer patterns inconsistent with individual use — the nested-account signature; (2) expanding VASP counterparty due diligence beyond entity-level lists and incorporation jurisdictions to activity-based screening — does this 'customer' address serve retail users at scale? The 46% licensing gap also confirms that jurisdiction of incorporation is not a reliable proxy for supervisory coverage, reinforcing the ESMA Interim MiCA Register as the authoritative source for EU CASP status (see feed-mica-17pct-licensed-21-days).
Source: [FATF — Understanding and Mitigating the Risks of Offshore VASPs](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Virtualassets/Understanding-Mitigating-Risks-Offshore-VASPs.html)
